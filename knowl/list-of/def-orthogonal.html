Definition 6.10 Orthogonal Vectors.

Two vectors \(\vec u,\vec v\) are orthogonal if \(\vec u\cdot\vec v=0\text{.}\)
🔗
This is equivalent to
🔗
\begin{equation*} \| \vec u+\vec v\|^2 = \|\vec u\|^2 + \|\vec v\|^2. \end{equation*}
Solution.
Proof: The equivalent statement, known as the Pythagorean Theorem, is true since
\begin{equation*} \|\vec u + \vec v\|^2 = (\vec u + \vec v)(\vec u + \vec v) = \|\vec u\|^2 + \|\vec v\|^2 + 2\vec u \cdot \vec v = \|\vec u\|^2 + \|\vec v\|^2 \end{equation*}
since \(\vec u \cdot \vec v = 0\) because the vectors are orthogonal.
